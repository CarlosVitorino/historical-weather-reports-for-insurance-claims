# Win on named stations, not on price

Weather Evidence enters a market where **$9.99 is already taken**. StormRecord sells almost exactly the same offer: one US address, one date, a free preview, an instant PDF, no account ([StormRecord](https://www.stormrecord.com/homeowner/)). HailPoint and WeatherGuidance sell hail lookups at $9.95 ([HailPoint](https://www.hailpoint.com/hail-report-by-address); [WeatherGuidance](https://www.weatherguidance.com/hailintel_verify_home.html)), and StormAuditor charges $2.99 ([StormAuditor](https://www.stormauditor.com/)). Price will not set us apart. Content will. StormRecord's sample report uses blended values with no named station, and it marks hail and lightning "Not queried". Mid-tier sellers charge $35–$50 for a single type of weather (hail, or wind, or lightning). That leaves one open position: **the only $9.99 report where every number names its NOAA/NWS station, with distance and bearing, and that combines warnings, Storm Events, radar hail and lightning for one date**. Organic search should be the main channel. The plan is a small set of honest guides plus storm-event pages built only where NCEI or SPC records exist, because no competitor has pages for individual storm dates. Google Ads can be tested but is mathematically fragile: at $9.99, the most we can pay per click and still break even is about **$0.47 if 5% of clicks buy, and about $0.93 if 10% buy**. Typical clicks cost $4–$5. So the first campaign should be a capped, long-tail test, not a growth engine. Two decisions belong to the founder before anything public goes live. First, **StormRecord's PDF is titled "Weather Evidence Report", which is our product name**. Second, the legal kill risk in the brief means words like "certified", "official" and "court-admissible" must never appear in our copy.

> **Founder decision needed: name collision.** StormRecord's 12-page PDF is titled **"Weather Evidence Report"**, with IDs like `SR-2026-02-19-KBMG-…` (see `docs/materials/StormRecord_Sample_Report.pdf.md`). StormRecord is the closest competitor at the same price. If we sell "Weather Evidence" reports, buyers may assume we are StormRecord, or that we copied them. StormRecord could also claim prior use of the phrase. None of the research checked USPTO filings or common-law use. Before spending on ads or building backlinks to weatherevidence.com, the founder should (1) search USPTO for "Weather Evidence" and run a quick clearance check with a trademark attorney, and (2) choose between keeping the name with a clearly distinct product title (for example "Station-Cited Weather Record") and renaming now, while switching costs almost nothing. This is a business and legal judgment, not something this report can settle.

## Three price bands, and the cheapest one is crowded

The US market has three rungs. **Instant per-address PDFs cost $2.99–$50.** **Contractor map subscriptions cost about $60 a month to $2,500 a year.** **Meteorologist or forensic reports cost $150 to $1,000 or more, or are quote-only.** Above all three sits the government's certified copy: NCEI charges **$180 for 1–40 pages** (the same page also says "most orders start at $191") ([NCEI certification](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/services/certification)). HailTrace shows the whole ladder in one company: a $40 "Weather History" report described as "not reviewed by a meteorologist", a $150 meteorologist-reviewed report, and a $1,000 certified forensic report ([HailTrace hail reports](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports); [HailTrace FAQ](https://hailtrace.com/faq)). HailStrike sells OneSite reports at $35/$45/$50 ([HailStrike order](https://hailstrike.com/onesite/order)). AnythingWeather sells HailFACTS and WindFACTS at $40 each and LightningFACTS at $199, one type of weather per report ([AnythingWeather store](https://store.anythingweather.com/weather_verification_reports)). Interactive Hail Maps charges $999–$1,999 a year and includes reports ([IHM pricing](https://www.interactivehailmaps.com/pricing-page/)). CompuWeather publishes no price and delivers signed reports from Certified Consulting Meteorologists in about five business days ([CompuWeather](https://compuweather.com/)). Carrier-side tools, Verisk Benchmark and Cotality (CoreLogic) Weather Verification, are sold inside Xactimate, ISO ClaimSearch and Guidewire, with no public price ([Verisk/Guidewire](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/verisk-introduces-benchmark-reports-via-guidewire-to-streamline-weather-related-claims); [Cotality WVS](https://www.wvs.corelogic.com/)). The brief puts all of that out of scope.

| Competitor | Entry offer | Price | Main audience | What it lacks vs. Weather Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| StormRecord | One address, one date, instant PDF | $9.99 web; $49.99 in iOS app ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stormrecord/id6758730551)) | Homeowners, construction | Blended Visual Crossing values, no named station, hail and lightning "Not queried", 3-year lookback |
| StormAuditor | Free lookup, full report | $2.99 | Homeowners | Estimated hail and wind only |
| HailPoint / WeatherGuidance | Hail history by address | $9.95 | Homeowners, contractors | Hail/wind history only, not built around a single date |
| StormerSite | 6-year, 3-mile hail history | $14.95 ([StormerSite](https://www.stormersite.com/hail-history-reports)) | Adjusters, contractors | Hail only |
| HailTrace | Weather History → Reviewed → Forensic | $40 / $150 / $1,000 | Contractors, adjusters | Not self-serve for homeowners; wind data only from 2020 |
| AnythingWeather | HailFACTS / WindFACTS / LightningFACTS | $40 / $40 / $199 | Policyholders, public adjusters, insurers, attorneys | Each type of weather sold separately |
| NCEI | Certified raw records | $180+ | Courts, attorneys | Not interpreted for an address |

The table shows where we can win and where we can't. StormRecord's own blog admits that NOAA data is free and that "there is no universal requirement to buy a weather report". Its About page and its report credit values "compiled by Visual Crossing", which are "not identical to a direct NOAA station download" ([StormRecord blog](https://stormrecord.com/blog/historical-weather-data-insurance-claim/); [StormRecord About](https://stormrecord.com/about/)). Our product's four improvements answer exactly those weaknesses, so they should lead every page and ad. A second fact also shapes positioning: HailTrace's $40 tier and AnythingWeather's $40-per-hazard pricing make our report **four times cheaper than the cheapest multi-source alternative a contractor already buys**. That is a stronger message for roofers and public adjusters than "same price as StormRecord".

## Homeowners and public adjusters buy single reports; carrier adjusters don't

Only StormRecord, StormAuditor and HailScore lead with homeowners. Most hail vendors are lead-generation tools for roofers, with per-address reports bolted onto canvassing maps ("Get There First") ([Interactive Hail Maps](https://www.interactivehailmaps.com/); [Hook Agency](https://hookagency.com/blog/door-knock-with-hailtrace/)). Vendors that sell to claims split their pages by audience. AnythingWeather has separate paths for attorneys, for "Policyholders & Public Adjusters", and for "Insurance Companies & Adjusters" ([AnythingWeather](https://www.anythingweather.com/solutions/insurance-and-legal/policyholders-public-adjusters/)). WeatherGuidance prices a Basic tier for homeowners and contractors and a $29.95 "Forensic" tier for adjusters, attorneys and engineers ([WeatherGuidance](https://www.weatherguidance.com/hailintel_verify_home.html)). The common pain is the **date of loss**: carriers check the claimed date against archived data, and the date of loss is the date of the storm, not the date the damage was found ([Estimate on Demand](https://estimateondemand.com/date-of-loss-prior-hail-damage-roof-claims/)). Verisk says about 30% of hail claims carry a wrong date of loss. That is the vendor's own figure, not independently checked ([Verisk](https://verisk.com/company/newsroom/xactware-and-verisk-climate-make-it-easier-to-validate-weather-related-claims)).

This changes the founder's test plan. The project brief says to sell to "claims adjusters". **Staff adjusters at carriers already get Benchmark or Cotality reports inside their claims software**, so they are the hardest group to reach with a self-serve $9.99 report. The likely buyers are, in order: **homeowners preparing or disputing a claim**, **public adjusters and independent field adjusters** who need a quick attachment for the file, and **small roofers** who want one claim document without a subscription. Plaintiff attorneys need certified data or a credentialed expert, so they are a poor fit ([CompuWeather attorneys](https://compuweather.com/services/attorneys/)). The message for each group follows from what competitors leave out. For homeowners: see what the public records say at your address on your loss date before you call your insurer. For adjusters: every number traces to a named station and an NCEI record, so you can answer the carrier's black-box radar report. Gen Re notes that competing automated reports often contradict each other, which is why carriers hire forensic meteorologists for disputes ([Gen Re](https://www.genre.com/us/knowledge/publications/2025/january/weather-or-not-to-use-a-forensic-meteorologist-in-the-claims-process-en)). A report the reader can reproduce from the sources is the honest middle ground between those black boxes and a $1,000 expert.

## Storm-event pages are the unclaimed SEO slot

Two of the six search query families carry buying intent: **"hail/storm report by address"** and **"weather report for insurance claim"**. Both have low volume and high intent. The high-volume families ("weather on [date]", "hail map [city]", "did it hail in [city]") are mostly informational, and free or programmatic sites win them. StormerSite has template pages for every city, year and day (`/hail_reports/{city}_{state}/{year}/{Month}/{day}`) ([StormerSite Denver 2024](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/denver_colorado/2024)). StormAuditor has a page for every state and city ([StormAuditor TX/Kyle](https://www.stormauditor.com/storm-data/tx/kyle)). The Old Farmer's Almanac has a page for every city and date ([Almanac](https://www.almanac.com/weather/history/PA/Wilkes-Barre/2025-08-28)). HailScore writes city-plus-year blog posts and a "Did hail hit my house?" landing page ([HailScore](https://www.myhailscore.com/did-hail-hit-my-address)). StormRecord, by contrast, relies on a few audience pages plus a how-to blog targeting "weather report for insurance claim" and "how insurance companies verify weather claims" ([StormRecord blog](https://stormrecord.com/blog/how-insurance-companies-verify-weather-claims/)). Results for question-style searches are thin. A search for "how to get NOAA weather data for insurance claim" turns up enterprise vendors and a webinar PDF, not a plain answer ([Cotality](https://www.cotality.com/platforms/weather-verify)). A search for "certified weather report for insurance claim cost" returns foreign news ([Gasgoo](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/articles/market-industry/stormy-proof-sells-for-auto-insurance-claims-in-beijing-1015347)). Sample PDFs rank on their own: Verisk's hail and lightning samples appear for "report by address" searches ([Verisk hail sample](https://www.verisk.com/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-hail-history-report-sample.pdf)).

Mass-produced pages carry a real risk. Google's August–September 2025 spam update targeted **scaled content abuse**, meaning large numbers of template pages that add little, whatever tool produced them ([Seeders](https://seeders.com/blog/august-2025-google-spam-update/)). StormerSite still ranks with pages that say "No hail reports were recorded" ([StormerSite Denver City TX](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/denver_city_texas)), but copying that pattern on a brand-new domain is the wrong bet. The opening nobody has taken is **one page per real storm event**, keyed by date and county, built only when NCEI Storm Events or SPC records exist. Those pages match how claim buyers think, because they already know their date of loss.

### Technical checklist for the developer

Serve every page as HTML from the server, with no content that needs JavaScript to appear. Put one canonical URL on each page, use lowercase slugs, and never let two URL spellings index the same page (the Almanac has duplicate encodings indexed, like `South+Boston` and `South%20Boston` ([Almanac](https://www.almanac.com/weather/history/VA/South+Boston/2025-08-28))). Return a 404 for county and date combinations with no records instead of a thin page that loads normally. Publish a sitemap index split by page type, with accurate `lastmod` dates, plus `robots.txt`. Mark address results and checkout pages noindex. For structured data, add `Product` + `Offer` at 9.99 USD on the product page (no fake ratings), `Organization` on the home page, `BreadcrumbList` on storm hubs, and possibly `Dataset` with `isBasedOn` pointing to NCEI/SPC on methodology and event pages. **FAQ rich results stopped appearing for everyone on 7 May 2026**, so FAQ markup will not earn extra space in results ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results/574429/)). Load maps lazily and cache event pages so they stay fast on cheap hosting.

## Legal wording: copy StormRecord's caution, not AnythingWeather's boldness

Competitors fall into two camps. Cautious sellers say outright what their report is not. StormRecord calls its report "an automated compilation, not a certified government copy or expert opinion", says acceptance "depends on the reviewing insurer", and says a county-level event "does not establish conditions at your property" ([StormRecord blog](https://stormrecord.com/blog/weather-report-for-insurance-claim/)). HailScore says its output is "not a damage diagnosis or an insurance determination… not legal advice" ([HailScore](https://www.myhailscore.com/blog/free-hail-damage-report-any-address)). Bold sellers make claims a $9.99 automated product cannot back. AnythingWeather markets HailFACTS as "insurance-approved in many cases" and says its reports "stand up in court" ([HailFACTS](https://www.anythingweather.com/products/hailwatch/hailfacts/)). HailTrace's expert-tier page refers to FRE 702/Daubert ([HailTrace](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports/meteorologist-expert)). "Certified" has a specific legal meaning: only NCEI, on behalf of the Department of Commerce, certifies US weather records "to meet the authentication requirements for records submitted as evidence in a court of law" ([NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/services/certification)). Courts decide admissibility by judging the expert and the method, not the label on a report. For example, N.D. Texas excluded a meteorologist's hail and wind opinions on reliability grounds ([N.D. Tex. order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txnd-3_16-cv-02255/pdf/USCOURTS-txnd-3_16-cv-02255-4.pdf)). Google's ad rules point the same way. Its misrepresentation policy bans hiding or misstating "affiliations or qualifications" and promising "an improbable result" ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6020955?hl=en)). Google has also said "We prohibit ads that mislead users by implying an affiliation with a government agency" ([The Markup](https://themarkup.org/google-the-giant/2021/05/13/ads-are-impersonating-government-websites-in-google-results-despite-ban)).

| Avoid (site, PDF, ads) | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Certified, certified weather report | "Not a certified NCEI record. Need one? Here's how ($180+)." |
| Official, NOAA report, government weather report | "Built from public NOAA/NWS records. Independent; not affiliated with NOAA or NWS." |
| Court-admissible, court-approved, Daubert-ready, litigation-grade | "Documents historical conditions; does not provide expert or legal opinions." |
| Insurance-approved, accepted by insurers | "Acceptance is at your insurer's discretion. Ask your adjuster what they need." |
| Proves hail damage, verified date of loss, causation | "Shows what public records report near your address on that date." |
| Forensic, expert, meteorologist-reviewed | "Every figure cites its station, distance and bearing." |
| Win/get your claim paid | "Evidence to attach to your claim file." |
| Free (for the paid report) | "Free preview, full PDF $9.99." |

Also, never use NOAA or NWS logos or seals, and never make the site look like a .gov page. Put the data-delay notes (Storm Events lags; SPC reports are preliminary) on the PDF itself, where they work as both a disclaimer and a sign of credibility. The word "Evidence" in our own brand already carries a legal meaning, which is one more reason to keep every other phrase modest. A lawyer should review the final disclaimer text, since the brief names legal issues as the risk that would stop the business.

## Google Ads works only below about $1 a click

The numbers set the limits. Assume about **$9.30 net per sale** after creem.io fees (an assumption: Creem's fee schedule was not checked). The highest cost per click (CPC) that still breaks even is net revenue × the share of clicks that buy:

| Clicks that buy | 2% | 5% | 10% | 15% | 20% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Break-even CPC | $0.19 | $0.47 | $0.93 | $1.40 | $1.86 |

The 2025 cross-industry average CPC was **$5.26–$5.42**, and ecommerce search averaged **$4.14** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-costs-keep-rising-but-conversion-rates-improved-in-2025-477927); [The Ad Spend](https://theadspend.com/blog/google-ads-search-ecommerce)). At those prices, about half of all visitors would have to buy just to break even. Searches that roofers bid on are worse: insurance-intent restoration terms run $5–$15 a click and rise **200–400% after storms**, exactly when our demand peaks ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/water-damage-restoration-seo/storm-damage-ppc-advertising-for-restoration-companies/); [Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/roofing-google-ads-storm-season)). Someone searching "hail report for my address" who sees a free preview may well buy 10–20% of the time, but that is unproven. Even at that rate, the ceiling is about $1–$2 a click. A higher order value would be the real fix: a pack of 10 reports for adjusters, or multi-date bundles. None of this changes the $9.99 entry price.

### Starter campaign

Start with **$10–$20 a day, US only, phrase and exact match only, manual CPC or Maximize Clicks with a $1.00 cap** (at most $1.50 on the tightest exact terms). The **evergreen campaign** has four ad groups: hail report by address/date; storm or wind report by address/date; weather report for insurance claim / date of loss; and long-tail past weather on a specific date. A second **storm-surge campaign** stays paused. When an SPC hail or wind cluster hits the launch region, turn it on with ZIP or radius targets (location option "Presence") for about 2–6 weeks. The 2–6 week window is a guess, not sourced. Do **not** raise bids into roofer-inflated auctions. A pre-built, paused storm campaign aimed at the affected ZIPs is how roofing agencies run storm response ([Elevarus](https://elevarus.com/roofing-storm-response-paid-search-playbook/)). An optional third ad group, "weather report for public adjusters", tests whether professionals convert. Judge each keyword after about 100–200 clicks. Pause any keyword whose Keyword Planner top-of-page bid is above $1.50 before it spends.

Starter negative keywords: forecast, radar, live, today, tomorrow, 10 day, hourly, app, tracker, alert, jobs, career, salary, degree, roofer, roofing, contractor, repair, replacement, near me, lawyer, attorney, insurance quote, cheap insurance, car insurance, auto, hurricane tracker, chaser, siren. Wait to add "free" until we see whether people searching for free tools still buy after the free preview. Review the search-terms report weekly.

Ad copy that stays within Google's rules: "Hail Report for Your Address – $9.99 PDF", "Weather on Your Loss Date, by Address", "Built From Public NOAA/NWS Records", "Every Reading Names Its Station", "Independent – Not a Gov't Site". Use sitelinks for Sample report, What's included, Need certified NCEI data?, and For adjusters. Use callouts for Instant PDF, Address-specific, Distance & bearing to station, and No subscription. Add a price asset at $9.99 and a structured snippet listing Hail, Wind, Lightning, Storm Events and NWS Warnings. Since October 2025, Google automatically adds a "Not a government website" label to ads in its government-documents category ([Kliken](https://help.kliken.com/en/articles/12461228-google-ads-policy-update-government-documents-and-services-disclosure-october-2025)). Ads that say "NOAA report" or "official" risk being put in that category. A few low-budget test ads before launch will show whether any labels or limited approvals appear.

### Tracking purchases through creem.io

Our checkout runs on Creem's domain, where Google's click ID (gclid) is not available, so a browser tag alone will miss sales ([Stape community](https://community.stape.io/t/google-ads-conversions-third-party-domain/3693)). The setup has five steps. (1) Turn on auto-tagging, and when a visitor lands, save gclid/gbraid/wbraid in a first-party cookie and the server session. (2) When the server creates the Creem checkout, put the click ID and our internal order reference in Creem's `metadata` field ([Creem quickstart](https://docs.creem.io/getting-started/quickstart)). (3) When a `checkout.completed` webhook arrives with a valid signature, record the sale: value 9.99 USD, Creem's order_id as the transaction ID ([Creem webhooks](https://docs.creem.io/code/webhooks)). Webhook retries stop after about a day, so run a daily check against Creem's records. (4) Upload the sale to Google as an offline click conversion within 90 days of the click, or as an enhanced conversion keyed on the hashed buyer email ([Google offline import](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7012522)). (5) Also fire a browser purchase tag on the HTTPS success page with the same order_id, so Google can remove duplicates. Treat the server upload as the main conversion. One blog reports that from June 2026 these uploads moved to Google's Data Manager API ([Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/google-ads-en/google-ads-offline-conversion-import-guide/)). Check that before building step 4.

## Conclusion

The research shows the product does not need a new category. It needs a **sharper version of a category StormRecord has already proven** people will buy. The brief's four improvements (named stations, distance and bearing, Storm Events, hail and lightning feeds) are exactly what tells a careful buyer apart from StormRecord's blended values. They also make the honest, legally safe language a selling point: "here is where every number came from" is more convincing than "certified". Customers will mostly come through organic search, not ads. Storm-event pages and plain-English guides cost nothing per click, while paid search only breaks even if most visitors buy. The first Ads test should be read as a measurement of what share of visitors buy, not as a way to acquire customers.

Two findings could change the plan more than any keyword choice. The brand name may be unusable if StormRecord's "Weather Evidence Report" title gives it a prior claim. And the founder's intended test buyer, the staff claims adjuster, is the group most firmly locked into carrier tools. Testing with public adjusters, independent adjusters and homeowners first, and offering a 10-report pack for professionals, gives the $9.99 product its best chance of showing real demand before any money goes into scale.
