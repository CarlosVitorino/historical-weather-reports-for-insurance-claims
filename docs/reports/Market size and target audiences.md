# Win contested claims before chasing every storm

Weather Evidence can realistically serve a **US market worth about $4–8 million a year at $9.99 per report**. That market is the 0.4–0.8 million weather claims a year where a non-carrier buyer has a reason to question the date or the weather at an address. Behind it sits a theoretical ceiling of about $28–44 million, which would require every weather-related loss in the country to buy one report. A realistic capture is **roughly $1K–$42K in gross revenue in year 1** from a single launch region, with about $8K as a base case, and **roughly $40K–$340K by year 3** if the product expands nationally and adds professional bundles, with about $120K as a base case. Every one of these figures is an estimate built from sourced claim counts and assumed conversion rates. No seller in this tier publishes sales, so the single most important number, how many people actually buy a $9.99 report, is still unknown. The best first buyers are **public adjusters and independent/CAT adjusters**, followed by **homeowners with a disputed or denied wind, hail or lightning claim**. They have both the motive and the money, and the four data improvements over StormRecord (named stations, distance and bearing, Storm Events, hail and lightning) serve them directly. Carrier staff adjusters, the buyers the brief names, are the hardest group to sell to, because their employers already pay for Verisk or Cotality reports. Roofers and attorneys bring real demand but also the most legal risk. Real estate, construction delays and similar uses need a different product shape (multi-year history, date ranges, hourly timelines), and a few small changes could unlock them. StormRecord already sells a near-identical $9.99 report, and Google Ads break even only below about $1 a click. Together these mean the year-1 numbers must come from organic search and direct outreach, not paid acquisition.

## The paid weather-evidence market is about $100M, and $9.99 reaches a sliver

The money in US weather evidence sits mostly where a $9.99 self-serve report cannot reach. Nobody publishes a total. Built up from vendor estimates, it is about **$70–200M a year [estimate]**: carrier enterprise tools (Verisk Benchmark, Cotality Weather Verify) at about $20–60M, forensic meteorology at about $30–85M, contractor hail-map subscriptions at about $15–40M, NCEI certified records at about $1–5M, and **cheap instant reports ($3–$50) at only about $2–10M**. The cheap tier is the least documented and is a guess bracket. The building blocks are sourced. Verisk's FY2024 revenue was $2.88B, but it does not break out Benchmark ([Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/verisk-reports-fourth-quarter-2024-and-full-year-2024-financial-results/)). HailTrace is estimated at under $5M revenue with about 52 staff ([ZoomInfo](https://zoominfo.com/c/hail-trace/356109358); [CoAdvantage](https://coadvantage.com/hubfs/Case%20Studies/Complete_with_Docusign_HailTrace_Case_Study_.pdf?hsLang=en)). Forensic meteorologists bill **$300–$550 an hour** ([Expert Institute](https://www.expertinstitute.com/expert/forensic-meteorology-expert-witness-e-471929/)), and only about **240–260 Certified Consulting Meteorologists** exist ([AccuWeather](https://dev-business.accuweather.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/08/AccuWeather-Forensics-Overview.pdf)). NCEI handles **over 2 million data requests a year, mostly free** ([Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2023-17803.pdf)), and charges **$180 for a certified order** ([NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/services/certification)). Syndicated "weather risk" market reports of $2–16B measure insurance premiums or forecasting, not verification. They should not be used as a TAM.

Demand is better counted in claims than in dollars. US homeowners lines produced **5.27M claims in 2025 and about 6.5M in 2024** ([Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2026/04/14/336889.htm)). About **2.8% of insured homes (1 in 35) file a wind/hail claim each year** ([Triple-I](https://iii.org/fact-statistic/facts-statistics-homeowners-and-renters-insurance)). Hail alone runs about **0.8–1.5M claims a year** across all lines, swinging ±30–50% between years ([NICB](https://nicb.org/media/1923/download); [Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/4aba4b/siteassets/media/campaigns/gated/claims/claimsearch-trends-report-h1-2024.pdf)). Lightning produced **61,986 homeowners claims in 2025, at an average of $26,616** ([Business Wire/Triple-I](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260618730348/en/Triple-I-Lightning-Caused-%241.65-Billion-in-US-Homeowners-Claim-Payouts-in-2025-Average-Cost-Per-Claim-Surges-Nearly-43)). The contested slice is what matters for a buyer outside the carrier. Verisk says **30–40% of hail claims carry an incorrect or inconsistent date of loss** ([Verisk Benchmark](https://www.verisk.com/insurance/products/benchmark/); [GlobeNewswire 2015](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2015/03/31/720595/0/en/xactware-and-verisk-climate-make-it-easier-to-validate-weather-related-claims.html)). Verisk sells a competing product, so treat that rate as a vendor claim. In 2023, **37.4% of 8.8M homeowners claims closed without payment** ([Weiss Ratings](https://weissratings.com/en/weiss-ratings-daily/homeowners-beware-big-insurers-deny-half-of-damage-claims)). That figure includes claims below the deductible, so it overstates disputes.

Two things stand out. First, even the high year-3 case is a **small business, not a venture-scale market**. The whole serviceable pool is worth a few million dollars, and $336K would be about 4–8% of it. Second, these ranges sit below the simpler "1–5% of 2–3M wind/hail claims buy a report" hypothetical ($0.2–1.5M). That hypothetical counts claims that carriers already verify with their own tools, so it overstates what a self-serve product can reach. The low year-1 case, about a hundred reports, is close to the brief's own milestone: "sell a handful against the existing StormRecord report".

## StormRecord already sells this, and paid clicks cost more than a sale earns

The capture rates above assume that Weather Evidence takes share in a tier that is already contested at the same price. **StormRecord sells one US address, one date, an instant PDF for $9.99**, and $49.99 in its iOS app ([StormRecord](https://www.stormrecord.com/homeowner/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stormrecord/id6758730551)). StormAuditor charges $2.99 ([StormAuditor](https://www.stormauditor.com/)), and HailPoint charges $9.95 ([HailPoint](https://www.hailpoint.com/hail-report-by-address)). The base year-3 case of $120K equals **about 1–6% of the $2–10M cheap-tier spend estimate**. That share is plausible only if the station-cited content clearly beats StormRecord's blended Visual Crossing values, which StormRecord itself says are "not identical to a direct NOAA station download" ([StormRecord About](https://stormrecord.com/about/)). For professionals, the stronger comparison is against the $40 single-hazard reports they already buy (HailFACTS, WindFACTS, HailTrace Weather History) ([AnythingWeather](https://store.anythingweather.com/weather_verification_reports); [HailTrace](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports)). Weather Evidence costs a quarter of that and covers all hazards.

Google Ads cannot buy this volume. Break-even cost per click is net revenue × conversion rate: about **$0.47 at 5% and $0.93 at 10%**. The 2025 cross-industry average CPC was **$5.26–$5.42**, and ecommerce search averaged **$4.14** ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ads-costs-keep-rising-but-conversion-rates-improved-in-2025-477927); [The Ad Spend](https://theadspend.com/blog/google-ads-search-ecommerce)). Storm-damage terms run **$5–$15 and spike after storms**, which is exactly when demand peaks ([PushLeads](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/water-damage-restoration-seo/storm-damage-ppc-advertising-for-restoration-companies/)). Apply that to the base year-1 case of 800 sales. At a 10% conversion rate, 800 sales need 8,000 clicks. At $4.14–$5.26 a click, that costs **$33K–$42K to earn about $7.4K net [E]**. So the year-1 scenarios assume **organic search plus direct outreach**, and Ads stay a capped test to measure conversion. A larger order is the only change that fixes the ad math. A 10-report pack priced at, for illustration, $79 (about $74 net [E]) breaks even at **about $2.20 a click at a 3% conversion rate and about $3.70 at 5%**. That comes close to real CPCs and does not touch the $9.99 entry price.

The evidence behind the top two rows is concrete. NAPIA reports PAs licensed in **46 states plus DC** ([propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/the-history-of-napia-and-its-importance-to-the-public-adjusting-profession/)). Florida's count runs from **2,100 resident PAs** ([FAPIA](https://www.fapia.net/nph.html)) to **3,738 active licenses** ([publicadjusterregistry.com](https://publicadjusterregistry.com/florida)). Florida caps PA fees at 20%, and at 10% in the year after a declared emergency ([publicadjusterauthority.com](https://publicadjusterauthority.com/public-adjuster-contingency-fee-limits-by-state)), so a $9.99 report is trivial against the fee. NAIIA describes itself as **250 adjusting firms** ([NAIIA](https://x.com/NAIIAdjuster)), and its 2023 deck lists 1,400+ adjusters and appraisers ([NAIIA deck](https://naiia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2023-NAIIA-Pitch-Deck.pdf)). An industry webinar called a **$39 hail report "a good starting point"** before a forensic meteorologist ([CLM](https://www.theclm.org/Magazine/articles/you-get-what-you-pay-for-weather-data-insurance-claims-webinar/2759)). WeatherGuidance sells a $29.95 "Forensic" tier to adjusters, attorneys and engineers ([WeatherGuidance](https://www.weatherguidance.com/hailintel_verify_home.html)). These buyers already pay three to four times our price for less.

The rows below rank 3 rest on weaker evidence. BLS counts **about 356,100 claims adjusters, examiners and investigators** across all lines ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/claims-adjusters-appraisers-examiners-and-investigators.htm)). Their employers buy Benchmark, which integrates with Xactimate and Guidewire ([Verisk](https://www.verisk.com/company/newsroom/verisk-introduces-benchmark-reports-via-guidewire-to-streamline-weather-related-claims)). That is why the brief's goal of seeing whether "claims adjusters pay" should be tested on **independent and public adjusters, not carrier staff**. The brief and the market point in slightly different directions here, and the founder should decide which group the first test targets. IBISWorld counts **about 105,876 roofing businesses** ([IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/roofing-contractors/198)), and GAF WeatherHub costs $349–649 a year ([GAF](https://gaf.com/en-us/for-professionals/tools/interactive-hail-map)). Many contractors already get unlimited address reports through subscriptions like these, so Weather Evidence wins only with roofers who don't subscribe, or on cases that need wind, rain and station data rather than hail alone. Geography sharpens the targeting. Texas had **about 23% of US hail claims** in 2017–19 ([NICB](https://nicb.org/node/20125)), Texas homeowners claims closed without payment about 47% of the time in 2024 ([Carroll County News](https://www.mycarrollcountynews.com/online_features/press_releases/article_70deac98-f950-5481-a69d-4bd3496d2029.html)), and SB 458 requires an appraisal clause in residential policies from January 2026 ([Donegan Law](https://donegan.com/blog/texas-insurance-appraisal-rules-sb-458-home-auto-claims/)). That makes North Texas a strong candidate for the single launch region.

## Non-insurance uses mostly need a different product shape

Outside homeowners claims, the one-address, one-date shape fits a few cases well and misses most. The best adjacent fit is **legal triage**. About **745K weather-related crashes a year cause 268K injuries** ([FHWA](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/weather/roadimpact.htm)), and snow-and-ice premises cases turn on the "storm in progress" defense, which needs hourly precipitation and temperature ([Smith, Gambrell & Russell](https://www.sgrlaw.com/articles/whether-or-not-a-weather-report-suffices/)). Lawyers check the weather cheaply before paying for certified records. One PI attorney said, "I can get climate certification papers for $20" ([Wisconsin Law Journal](https://wislawjournal.com/?p=21034)), and regional climate centers certify for **$25–$50** ([MRCC](https://www.mrcc.purdue.edu/data_serv/customOrdering.jsp)). That shows the ceiling on what this segment will pay: a $9.99 triage report is an easy purchase, but the certified alternative is cheap too.

The poor fits are clear. **Real estate due diligence** has a large pool, **4.06M existing-home sales a year** ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2026/01/14/2025-existing-home-sales-miss-2024-pace-by-razor-thin-1k-margin/)), but buyers and inspectors want a multi-year hail history, which already sells for $15–40 ([EDT Engineers](https://www.edtengineers.com/blog-post/evaluation-hail-history-reports)). **Construction delay claims** compare many project days against a "normal" baseline drawn from the nearest NOAA station, often a 10-year average ([Berger Singerman](https://www.bergersingerman.com/news-insights/what-you-need-to-know-about-construction-weather-delay-claims); [Cordulus](https://www.cordulus.com/blog/weather-clause-in-construction-contract-example)). A single date fits only one-event disputes, such as a wind event that topples formwork. **Event cancellation** is parametric and verified by the insurer at a named station ([TMHCC](https://www.tmhcc.com/en-us/products/contingency/event-weather)). **Workers' comp heat cases** are few, about 3,389 days-away cases a year ([OSHA](https://osha.gov/heat-exposure/rulemaking/background)). **Solar warranties** mostly exclude hail ([SolarReviews](https://www.solarreviews.com/blog/solar-panel-hail-damage-what-you-need-to-know)), so those losses land back in homeowners claims. **Trucking fleets** are enterprise buyers who want integrations. **Crop insurance** works per field over a season and is out of scope.

| Use case | Fits as built? | Small change that unlocks it | Within the brief? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disputed date of loss (hail and wind) | Partly: the buyer must know the date | **Multi-date bundle** (for example, the 3–7 days around the claimed date in one PDF) | Yes, same public inputs |
| PA / IA / engineer files | Yes | **10-report pack** of prepaid credits, no account API | Yes; a pack is not an insurer integration |
| Slip-and-fall, auto PI triage | Partly: daily data is too coarse | **Hourly temperature, precipitation type and ice timeline** for the date and the prior 24–72h, from GHCNh station data | Yes, data already in the GHCNh input |
| Lightning and surge disputes | Yes, if the lightning feed is solid | None | Yes |
| Real-estate roof history | No | **Multi-year hail/wind history mode** from NCEI Storm Events and SPC reports | Probably, but confirm it stays inside the "three free public inputs" |
| Construction delays | No | **Date-range "weather days" summary** against a NOAA normals baseline | Only if NOAA normals count as an allowed public input; check with the founder |
| Event, heat, trucking, solar, crop | No | None worth building | Trucking needs integrations (do-not-build) |

None of these changes involves forecasts, international data, proprietary feeds, insurer APIs, expert testimony or marketing material. The multi-date bundle and the 10-report pack do the most for the core market. The first answers the 30–40% date-of-loss problem directly. The second is the only change that improves the paid-acquisition math.

## Florida's adjuster definition and admissibility rules shape who can be sold to

The brief names legal issues as the condition that would stop the project. The market research locates two specific exposures. The first is **Florida's 2023 law (Ch. 2023-130), which broadened "public adjuster" to cover anyone other than an attorney who files a claim on an insured's behalf** ([Florida CFO](https://myfloridacfo.com/division/agents/insurance-insights/details/2023/05/31/in-the-know-summer-2023)). Selling a document to a roofer is not the problem. The problem would be marketing that tells contractors or homeowners the report "gets your claim paid" or helps someone file a claim for another person. Copy aimed at contractors must say "documentation" and never "claim filing" or "claim help".

The second is **admissibility**. Only NCEI certifies US weather records to meet court authentication requirements ([NCEI](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/services/certification)). New York appellate courts have held uncertified weather-website printouts inadmissible ([NY Appellate Digest](https://www.newyorkappellatedigest.com/?p=11524)), and courts judge expert method under reliability standards ([N.D. Tex. order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-txnd-3_16-cv-02255/pdf/USCOURTS-txnd-3_16-cv-02255-4.pdf)). For the attorney segment, then, Weather Evidence is a triage product that tells the buyer whether to order the $180 certified record or hire an expert. It must never say "certified", "official" or "court-admissible". Florida's litigation boom is also fading as a demand source: its share of US homeowners lawsuits fell from **79% in 2020 to 41% in 2025** after reforms ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/florida-homeowners-insurance-lawsuit-share-drops-to-41-after-reform-push-583458.aspx)). That is one more reason to start in Texas, not Florida. A lawyer should review the disclaimer and the copy aimed at contractors before any public launch.

## Five numbers decide whether the funnel is right, and each is cheap to measure

Every capture estimate rests on unknowns that research could not resolve. The biggest is the **preview-to-purchase conversion rate**. No public benchmark exists for $5–$15 instant reports, and no cheap-report seller publishes sales.

| Unknown | Why it matters | Cheapest way to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Preview-to-purchase conversion rate | Drives every capture scenario and the ad break-even | Run 100–200 capped clicks ($1 max CPC, about $100–200) and measure organic preview traffic. Count previews against sales |
| Whether PAs and IAs will pay, and buy packs | Decides the rank-1 and rank-2 segments | Email 30–50 Texas/Florida PAs from license lookups and the NAPIA directory with a free sample report; offer a 10-pack. Count replies and paid orders |
| Search volume for "report by address" and "weather report for insurance claim" | Sets the organic ceiling | Google Keyword Planner, US, free with an Ads account; record volumes and top-of-page bid ranges |
| Legal demand for weather records | Sizes the attorney triage segment | Ask NCEI (ncei.orders@noaa.gov) for annual certified-order counts; FOIA if refused |
| Distinct PA and IA headcount | Tightens rank-1 and rank-2 sizing | NIPR and state DFS/TDI license lookups for TX and FL |
| StormRecord's traction | Shows whether the tier has buyers | Google Ads Transparency Center, App Store review counts, sitemap growth over time |
| Neutral date-of-loss error rate | The SAM depends on Verisk's vendor-sourced 30–40% | Ask early PA buyers what share of their files have a disputed date |
| Net revenue per sale | Changes every break-even figure | Check Creem's fee schedule |

## Conclusion

The research turns "how big is the market" into a narrower and more useful question: **how many of the roughly half-million contested weather claims a year have a non-carrier buyer who will pay $9.99 to see the record?** Weather Evidence does not need a large market to succeed at its first milestone. It needs a few dozen professionals who already pay $30–$40 for single-hazard reports to switch. The total market is too small to support paid growth at $9.99. The plan that follows from the evidence is to lead with public and independent adjusters in one Texas hail region, add packs and multi-date bundles to raise order value, and let organic search bring homeowners. Carrier adjusters, contractors in claim-filing roles and courtroom uses should stay out of the first test.

One assumption in the brief should be revisited before the first sale. "See if claims adjusters pay" will probably return a false negative if it means carrier staff, who never buy their own tools. Running the test on public adjusters gives the product a fair chance, and it measures the segment where the real money is.
