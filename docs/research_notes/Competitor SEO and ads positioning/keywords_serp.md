# Organic search (SEO) landscape for address-and-date historical weather evidence (US, 2026)

Method note: research done 2026-10-15 via web search only. Direct page fetches (WebFetch) failed with DNS errors for competitor sites and developers.google.com, and Google Autocomplete (suggestqueries.google.com) was blocked by the proxy (HTTP 403). So the SERP observations below are what a search engine returned for each query, not a logged-in Google SERP with ads or People Also Ask boxes. I found no verified keyword volumes for the core queries. Treat every volume number as an unverified vendor claim.

## 1. Query families, rough volume and intent

### Takeaway
There are about six query families. The two with clear buying intent are "hail/storm report by address for insurance" and "weather report for insurance claim". They are low-volume and high-intent, and the people searching them are homeowners, public adjusters and roofers. The large-volume families ("weather history for a date", "hail map [city]", "did it hail in [city]") are mostly informational, and free sources rank for them. No public source gives reliable volume for the core "by address" terms.

### Cited Findings
- **Transactional "report by address" family** (hail report by address, hail history by address, storm/hail verification report). The results are almost all paid vendors: StormerSite hail history reports ([stormersite.com](https://stormersite.com/hail-history-reports)), the Verisk Benchmark Hail History sample ([verisk.com PDF](https://www.verisk.com/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-hail-history-report-sample.pdf)), AnythingWeather "Hail Verification Done Right" ([anythingweather.com](https://www.anythingweather.com/?p=119)) and a free BuildFolio "hail history by address" tool ([build-folio.com](https://build-folio.com/hail-history-by-address/)). Progressive's catastrophe-event pages (e.g. [Tulsa Hail 2025](https://wp-progressiveresponds-prod-main.apps.progressive.com/catastrophe-events/2025/may/tulsa-hail/)) also show up. That means insurer-owned event pages compete for these queries.
- The BuildFolio free tool lists NOAA hail events near an address with no signup and asks for an email to unlock the "full report". This is a lead-gen pattern that competes directly with a $9.99 product — [build-folio.com](https://build-folio.com/hail-history-by-address/)
- **"Weather report for insurance claim" / pricing family.** The results showed AnythingWeather "Severe Weather Verification Reports" aimed at policyholders and public adjusters ([anythingweather.com](https://www.anythingweather.com/solutions/insurance-and-legal/policyholders-public-adjusters/)), WeatherGuidance "Hail & Wind Damage Reports by Address" ([weatherguidance.com](https://www.weatherguidance.com/hailintel_verify_home.html)), StormAuditor "Free Hail, Wind & Storm History by Address" ([stormauditor.com](https://www.stormauditor.com/)), Titan Weather "Property Weather Report by Address" ([titanweather.com](https://titanweather.com/)), HailTrace "Weather History Report" ([hailtrace.com](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports/weather-history)) and OneClickCode weather history ([oneclickcode.com](https://www.oneclickcode.com/features/weather-history)). Search snippets show prices from about $2.99 per report to a $9.95 "Basic Report" (3-year history) to $40, and lookback periods of 3, 10 and 30 years. I could not open the pages to tie each price to a vendor — [search results summary, same URLs]
- **"Certified weather report" family.** The query returned no US price list. Results were mostly foreign (a Beijing weather-bureau certificate) or generic insurance news. A CLM magazine article says many private consulting firms do location- and date-specific forensic weather analysis — [theclm.org](https://www.theclm.org/Magazine/articles/its-not-nice-to-fool-mother-nature/188); [gasgoo Beijing](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/articles/market-industry/stormy-proof-sells-for-auto-insurance-claims-in-beijing-1015347). Inference: the SERP is weak and unsatisfying, which is an opening. Note too that "certified" carries legal and claim risk for a $9.99 product (see section 4).
- **"Weather history for a specific date" family** (informational, very broad). The Old Farmer's Almanac ranks with programmatic city×date pages, e.g. `almanac.com/weather/history/PA/Wilkes-Barre/2025-08-28`, with many URL variants indexed. A local-TV how-to (Boston 25) and a WeatherHistory iOS app listing also rank — [almanac.com](https://www.almanac.com/weather/history/PA/Wilkes-Barre/2025-08-28); [boston25news.com](https://www.boston25news.com/weather/find-out-the-history-of-the-weather-by-place-and-date/29180223); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/weatherhistory/6758732320). Almanac pages are station-based (about 1,300 US/Canada stations) and often show "No data" for wind — same sources.
- **"Wind speed on a specific date" family.** Visual Crossing ranks with its own help articles ("Where can I find historical wind data?", "How do I find old weather data?"). They explain that daily wind is the maximum of hourly two-minute averages and route users to its Weather History page and API — [visualcrossing.com](https://www.visualcrossing.com/resources/documentation/weather-data/where-can-i-find-historical-wind-data/); [visualcrossing.com](https://www.visualcrossing.com/resources/documentation/weather-data/how-do-i-find-old-weather-data/). Timeanddate and Weather Underground history pages did not show up in my results. That is a gap, not evidence that they don't rank.
- **"Hail map [city]" / "hail history [city]" / "did it hail in [city] yesterday" family.** StormerSite takes most of the results with programmatic pages: `stormersite.com/hail_reports/{city}_{state}`, `/{city}_{state}/{year}`, `/{city}_{state}/{year}/{Month}/{day}` and `/showreport/{id}` — [Denver CO 2024](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/denver_colorado/2024); [Maple Lake MN 2004-04-18](https://stormersite.com/hail_reports/maple_lake_minnesota/2004/April/18); [Dallas TX](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/dallas_texas). For "did it hail in Dallas yesterday", local TV (Fox 4 forecast videos), CBS News and Cotality Hazard HQ event posts also ranked — [fox4news.com](https://www.fox4news.com/video/1724357); [cotality.com](https://www.cotality.com/uk/hazard-hq/giant-hail-strikes-the-dallas-fort-worth-region-and-southwest-missouri). HailDrive has storm-event pages (`haildrive.com/storms/in-miami-denver-2026-04-22`) — [haildrive.com](https://haildrive.com/storms/in-miami-denver-2026-04-22).
- **"NOAA storm events lookup" family** (navigational). NOAA/NCEI and data.gov catalog pages rank: [NCEI metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc:C00510/html), [catalog.data.gov](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/ncdc-storm-events-database), the [NOAA Climate Toolkit page](https://prod-01-alb-toolkit-climate.woc.noaa.gov/tool/storm-events-database), plus third-party explainers and roofer pages (e.g. [iowaroof.com Iowa hail data](https://www.iowaroof.com/iowa-hail-data)). The database is filterable by state, county and event type and lags real time by roughly 90 to 120 days (NOAA sources disagree) — same sources.
- **"Lightning strike report by address" family.** The top results are Verisk's Benchmark Lightning Strike Report sample (CG strikes within 5 mi, ±36 h around noon on the date of loss), lightning-claim guides from claim-help sites, and a VDE homeowner guide noting that detection-network locations can be disputed — [verisk.com PDF](https://www.verisk.com/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-lightning-strike-report-sample.pdf); [insuranceclaimrecoverysupport.com](https://insuranceclaimrecoverysupport.com/lighting-property-damage-insurance-claims/); [vde-verlag.de](https://www.vde-verlag.de/proceedings-en/456416175.html).
- **"How to get NOAA weather data for insurance claim" family.** NOAA has no official how-to in the results. They show B2B vendors (Verisk Benchmark Accelerator, Cotality Weather Verify, Synoptic Data insurance) and a Cornell NRCC webinar PDF — [verisk.com](https://www.verisk.com/496586/siteassets/media/downloads/claims/access-property-specific-weather-data-for-faster-claim-validation-benchmark-accelerator.pdf); [cotality.com](https://www.cotality.com/platforms/weather-verify); [synopticdata.com](https://synopticdata.com/solutions/insurance); [nrcc.cornell.edu](https://www.nrcc.cornell.edu/workshops/webinars/2025/01/present_1.pdf).
- **Adjacent volume (roofing).** These are unverified agency figures: "hail damage roof denver" about 880/mo ([coloradowebimpressions.com](https://coloradowebimpressions.com/seo-for-roofing)), "hail damage roof repair" about 500/mo US ([emulent.com](https://emulent.com/resources/roofing-seo-keywords)), "roof damage repair" about 6,600/mo ([pushleads.com](https://pushleads.com/restoration-company-seo/storm-damage/storm-damage-restoration-keywords/)). The same sources say "[city] hail damage" searches spike 400–1,200% in the 24–48 h after a hail event, but they cite no data behind it — same sources.

### Inferences
- Search volume is spiky and tied to events. Demand concentrates in the 1–14 days after a storm in a given metro, and again when a claim is disputed (weeks to months later).
- The family that fits the product best, "[hail|storm|wind] report by address for insurance", probably has low total volume (hundreds to low thousands per month across variants), but it converts well. The large informational families are mainly useful as entry points to the $9.99 report.
- Insurers (Progressive event pages) and enterprise data firms (Verisk, Cotality) rank for these queries. Their pages are aimed at adjusters, not policyholders, and they have no self-serve checkout. That leaves the consumer and public-adjuster self-serve slot open, but StormerSite, HailTrace, StormAuditor, Titan Weather, WeatherGuidance and AnythingWeather already compete for it.

### Gaps
- No verified volumes for any core term (Keyword Planner, Ahrefs and Semrush pages could not be reached, and autocomplete was blocked).
- Couldn't see live Google SERP features (ads, PAA, AI Overviews) or per-query ranking positions.
- Couldn't confirm which vendor charges $2.99, $9.95 or $40, because the vendor pages were unreachable.

## 2. What ranks per family, and which page types win

### Takeaway
Four page types win. Programmatic city/year/date data pages win "hail [city]" and "weather on [date]". Vendor product or landing pages win "report by address". Government dataset pages win the NOAA navigational queries. Guides from law firms, contractors and public adjusters win the "how to prove" questions.

### Cited Findings
- **Programmatic data pages:** StormerSite city, year and day hail pages use the template "All Hail Reports near {City}, {State} in {Year}". Each page has a "last time it hailed" date, count and max size within 10 mi, a monthly frequency chart and a severity chart, built from SPC reports since 2004 — [Denver CO 2025](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/denver_colorado/2025); [Lake City IA](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/lake_city_iowa). The Almanac uses `/weather/history/{ST}/{City}/{YYYY-MM-DD}` — [almanac.com](https://www.almanac.com/weather/history/VA/Oakton/2025-08-28).
- **Tool and landing pages:** BuildFolio's free address tool ([build-folio.com](https://build-folio.com/hail-history-by-address/)), StormAuditor ([stormauditor.com](https://www.stormauditor.com/)), Titan Weather ([titanweather.com](https://titanweather.com/)), HailTrace weather history ([hailtrace.com](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports/weather-history)) and WeatherGuidance verify ([weatherguidance.com](https://www.weatherguidance.com/hailintel_verify_home.html)).
- **Sample-report PDFs rank:** Verisk's sample hail and lightning report PDFs show up for "hail report by address" and "lightning strike report by address" — [hail sample](https://www.verisk.com/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-hail-history-report-sample.pdf); [lightning sample](https://www.verisk.com/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-lightning-strike-report-sample.pdf).
- **Guides:** Results for "how do I prove hail damage" are mostly public-adjuster and contractor blog content (e.g. Noble PA Group, which has many near-duplicate "Beginner's Guide" posts, some on a `devsite.` subdomain), plus a coverage-law blog on disputed hail dates — [noblepagroup.com](https://noblepagroup.com/?p=56026); [propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/which-hailstorm-damaged-your-roof-the-time-of-hail-damage-is-often-disputed/).
- **Official climatology pages** also rank for "hail history": NWS Tulsa/Fort Smith hail climatology ([weather.gov/tsa](https://www.weather.gov/tsa/climo_fsmhail)) and NWS Hanford's Bakersfield hail PDF ([weather.gov](https://weather.gov/media/hnx/BFlhail.pdf)).
- **App-store listings** (Hail Yeah, Hail Trace, Hail Recon) show up for "did it hail at address" queries. Hail Yeah allows 3 free address lookups a day, with unlimited on Pro, and uses SPC reports — [mwm.ai Hail Yeah](https://mwm.ai/apps/hail-maps-hail-yeah/6761221797); [Hail Recon](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/hail-recon/602877045?country=us).

### Inferences
- To rank for "report by address", a page has to offer an immediate free preview (the BuildFolio, StormAuditor and Hail Yeah pattern) before asking for payment. A pure paywall page would compete poorly with them.
- A public, real sample PDF is an asset that ranks and a trust signal (Verisk's samples rank on their own).

### Gaps
- Exact ranking order was not observed, and I could not check HailTrace's or Interactive Hail Maps' city-page footprint.

## 3. Programmatic SEO patterns and scaled-content risk

### Takeaway
City × year × date hail pages built from SPC/NOAA data (StormerSite, HailDrive, Almanac) are the established pattern and clearly rank. Since 2024–2025, Google's scaled content abuse policy targets mass-produced pages made "primarily to manipulate rankings" that add little value. Thousands of template pages that say "no hail recorded" are the main risk.

### Cited Findings
- Google ran a spam update starting 26 Aug 2025 (complete about 22 Sep 2025). Third-party summaries say it targeted scaled content abuse, expired-domain abuse and site-reputation abuse, and that the policy applies regardless of method (AI, human or scraped) — [seeders.com](https://seeders.com/blog/august-2025-google-spam-update/); [prioritypixels.co.uk](https://prioritypixels.co.uk/blog/googles-august-2025-spam-update-complete/); [seo-kreativ.de](https://www.seo-kreativ.de/en/blog/google-august-2025-spam-update-officially-complete/).
- One guide argues programmatic pages are safe when each page carries original data, context or evidence. A widely repeated "20–30% unique content" threshold has no Google source — [bulkbase.ai](https://bulkbase.ai/seo/understanding-googles-scaled-content-abuse-policy); [beyondindigopets.com](https://www.beyondindigopets.com/blog/how-google-is-cracking-down-on-scaled-content-abuse/).
- Sources disagree on when the scaled-content policy started (March 2024 vs. a January 2025 enforcement) — same sources. Google's own spam-policy page could not be fetched (DNS failure).
- Evidence of thin pages in the wild: StormerSite has pages like "No hail reports were recorded within 10 miles of Denver City, Texas in 2026" and many tiny towns × years, and they are still indexed and ranking — [stormersite.com Denver City TX](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/denver_city_texas); [Dallas OR 2026](https://www.stormersite.com/hail_reports/dallas_oregon/2026).
- Almanac city×date pages often show "No data" for fields, and multiple URL encodings of the same page are indexed (`South+Boston` vs `South%20Boston`), which is a duplicate-content smell — [almanac.com](https://www.almanac.com/weather/history/VA/South+Boston/2025-08-28); [almanac.com](https://www.almanac.com/weather/history/VA/South%20Boston/2025-08-28).

### Inferences
- A safer pattern for weatherevidence.com: publish **storm-event pages** (one per NOAA Storm Events / SPC event day × affected county or metro) only when there are records. Each should carry real content: report list with times, sizes and gusts, nearest ASOS station observations, a map and distances, and a CTA to "check your exact address". Don't generate every city × every date. Noindex or skip zero-event combinations.
- Keep one canonical URL per entity, and normalize slugs to avoid the Almanac encoding duplicates.
- Pages should launch as the 90–120-day NCEI lag clears or from preliminary SPC data, and be labeled preliminary vs. final. That is a real point of difference and keeps the pages "helpful".

### Gaps
- No direct quote from Google Search Central's spam policy text (fetch failed). Re-check https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies before building.
- No data on whether StormerSite-style sites lost traffic in the 2025 updates.

## 4. Opportunities: under-served long tail, snippet and PAA questions

### Takeaway
The best openings are question-format and dispute-oriented queries where today's results are vendor marketing, contractor content farms or foreign pages. Examples: "how to get NOAA weather data for insurance claim", "certified weather report for insurance claim cost", "insurance says no hail on my date", "wind speed at my address on [date]" and "lightning strike near my house report". Also hyper-local "storm on [date] in [county]" pages, produced only when events exist.

### Cited Findings
- No NOAA how-to ranks for "how to get NOAA weather data for insurance claim". The results are enterprise vendors and a webinar PDF — [verisk.com](https://www.verisk.com/496586/siteassets/media/downloads/claims/access-property-specific-weather-data-for-faster-claim-validation-benchmark-accelerator.pdf); [cotality.com](https://www.cotality.com/platforms/weather-verify).
- Results for "certified weather report for insurance claim cost" are off-target (Beijing, NZ, UK claims news), so there is no US answer page — [gasgoo](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/articles/market-industry/stormy-proof-sells-for-auto-insurance-claims-in-beijing-1015347); [insurancebusinessmag.com](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/nz/news/breaking-news/cost-of-weather-insurance-claims-surges-70-in-three-years-88736.aspx).
- Contractor and adjuster sources say date-of-loss disputes are the core pain. Most states require showing damage happened during the policy period, and insurers challenge hail dates — [propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/which-hailstorm-damaged-your-roof-the-time-of-hail-damage-is-often-disputed/). A vendor says some insurers pay hail claims only with a same-day hail report (vendor claim) — [stormersite.com](https://stormersite.com/hail-history-reports).
- Lightning claims are disputed over detection-network location accuracy — [vde-verlag.de](https://www.vde-verlag.de/proceedings-en/456416175.html). That supports content explaining distance and bearing from the address to each strike or report.
- For ASOS wind, a StormProof listing notes that adjusters, PAs and forensic engineers routinely cite ASOS observations — [clawhub.ai](https://clawhub.ai/oasiseng/stormproof).
- Verisk's sample shows how the report can contradict a claimed date — [verisk.com PDF](https://www.verisk.com/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-hail-history-report-sample.pdf). That opens content like "what to do if the hail report doesn't match your date of loss".
- Candidate PAA-style questions seen across the results: "how do I prove hail damage to my insurance company", "how to get NOAA weather data for insurance claim", "how far back does hail history go", "what is the Storm Events Database delay", "does insurance accept hail reports", "what size hail damages a roof" — drawn from [noblepagroup.com](https://noblepagroup.com/?p=52003), [NCEI metadata](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/metadata/geoportal/rest/metadata/item/gov.noaa.ncdc:C00510/html) and [anythingweather.com](https://www.anythingweather.com/solutions/insurance-and-legal/policyholders-public-adjusters/). The actual Google PAA boxes were not observed.

### Inferences
- High-value guide targets: (1) "How to get NOAA weather records for an insurance claim (step by step, free)", which is honest, links to NCEI, and offers the $9.99 report as the shortcut; (2) "Storm Events Database vs SPC reports vs ASOS: which evidence adjusters use"; (3) "Hail report doesn't match my date of loss"; (4) "Wind speed at an address on a specific date"; (5) "Lightning strike near my house: how to check".
- Avoid the word "certified" in titles unless the product really is certified. Target "certified weather report" with an explainer ("what 'certified' means, NCEI certified copies vs. private reports") instead. This ties to the brief's legal risk.

### Gaps
- Real PAA, autocomplete and AI Overview content could not be captured. Validate in a live browser or with Keyword Planner before committing.

## 5. Technical SEO basics for a small FastAPI-rendered site

### Takeaway
Render pages server-side as HTML (no JS-only content) and keep them fast. FAQPage markup no longer produces rich results (fully removed May 2026). Use Product/Offer for the $9.99 report, Dataset or Article/BreadcrumbList for storm-event pages, and sitemaps segmented by page type.

### Cited Findings
- Google limited FAQ rich results to authoritative government and health sites in Aug 2023. As of 7 May 2026, FAQ rich results no longer appear for anyone, the Search Console report is being dropped (June 2026) and API support is ending (Aug 2026). Google says it still uses FAQPage markup to understand pages, and that there is no need to remove it — [techwyse.com](https://www.techwyse.com/news/ai-search/google-faq-rich-results-deprecated-2026); [searchenginejournal.com](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results/574429/); [searchengineland.com](https://searchengineland.com/?p=430452); [developers.google.com FAQPage doc](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/faqpage).
- NCEI Storm Events bulk CSVs are reportedly downloadable without an API key (third-party write-up, not verified against NCEI) — [ai-analytics.org](https://ai-analytics.org/writing/noaa-storm-events-database/). This makes server-side pre-generation of event pages practical.

### Inferences
- **Structured data:**
  - `Product` + `Offer` (price 9.99 USD) on the report product page. Avoid fake `AggregateRating`.
  - `Dataset` on storm-event or methodology pages that describe NOAA-derived data, with `isBasedOn` linking to NCEI/SPC.
  - `BreadcrumbList` on state › county › event pages, and `Organization` on the home page.
  - FAQPage is optional (understanding only, no rich result).
  - Dataset and Product eligibility were not confirmed by fetching Google docs in this session.
- **FastAPI:** use Jinja2 server-rendered templates, set canonical tags, and return 404/410 for empty combinations instead of thin 200 pages. Generate `sitemap.xml` as a sitemap index split by type (guides, events, states) with accurate `lastmod`. Add `robots.txt`, and noindex address-specific results and checkout pages (they are personal data and duplicate-like).
- **Speed:** static CSS, no heavy map JS above the fold (lazy-load the map), and cache event pages (pre-render or HTTP cache headers) so Core Web Vitals pass on cheap hosting.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the current Google docs for Dataset and Product (merchant listing vs product snippet) eligibility, or the Core Web Vitals thresholds. Verify on developers.google.com.
