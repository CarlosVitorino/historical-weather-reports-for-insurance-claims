# Professional buyer segments for a $9.99 per-address historical weather report (US, 2024–2026)

Scope note: research was done with web search on 2026-10-15. Many search results were vendor marketing or aggregators; reliability is flagged on each line. Direct fetch of bls.gov failed (DNS error), so the BLS figures come from search snippets of the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook page. Anything marked **(estimate)** is my inference, not a sourced number.

## Q1. Public adjusters: headcount, caseload, tools, reachability

### Takeaway
No source gives a current national count of licensed public adjusters (PAs). Florida alone has about 2,100 to 3,700, depending on how you count. PAs are licensed in 46 states plus DC. They work on contingency (typically 10–20% of the settlement), so a $9.99 report is a trivial cost to them. They are a strong early-adopter segment, reachable through NAPIA and state associations such as FAPIA.

### Cited Findings
- NAPIA says PAs are now fully licensed in 46 of 50 states plus DC, up from about 33 states roughly 15 years earlier (self-reported). — [NAPIA history, propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/the-history-of-napia-and-its-importance-to-the-public-adjusting-profession/)
- Florida had several hundred licensed PAs before the 2004 storms, which grew to over 3,000 after Hurricane Wilma (2005). — [propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com, "public adjusters part two"](https://www.propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/blog/public-adjusters-part-two/)
- Florida had 2,914 licensed PAs as of June 2009 (state audit, OPPAGA). — [OPPAGA Report 10-06](https://oppaga.fl.gov/Documents/Reports/10-06.pdf)
- The Florida PA count today is uncertain. FAPIA says 2,100 licensed PAs reside in Florida ([FAPIA](https://www.fapia.net/nph.html)). A third-party registry lists 3,738 active Florida licensees ([publicadjusterregistry.com](https://publicadjusterregistry.com/florida)). The gap is likely residents vs. all active licenses, including non-resident licenses, but neither source explains it.
- NAPIA was founded in 1951 with 54 PAs from 35 firms. I found no current membership count. NAPIA runs a "find a public adjuster" member directory. — [NAPIA news](https://www.napia.com/news/napia-members-mobilize-to-support-property-owners-impacted-by-hurricane-francine-in-louisiana-and-su)
- In August 2024 the Florida DFS publicly recognized the value of licensed PAs after Hurricane Debby. — [DFS press release PDF](https://propertyinsurancecoveragelaw.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/DFS-press-release.pdf)
- PA fees: commonly 5–20% of the settlement, with residential claims often 10–15%. Florida caps fees at 20% for standard claims and 10% for claims in the first year after a declared emergency. Texas caps fees at 10%. Sources conflict for IL, CA and NY. — [publicadjusterauthority.com fee limits](https://publicadjusterauthority.com/public-adjuster-contingency-fee-limits-by-state); [publicadjusterlistings.com](https://www.publicadjusterlistings.com/guides/public-adjuster-cost) (non-authoritative sites)
- Florida 2023 law (Ch. 2023-130) broadened the definition of "public adjuster" to cover anyone other than an attorney who files a claim for an insured. — [Florida CFO, "In The Know – Summer 2023"](https://myfloridacfo.com/division/agents/insurance-insights/details/2023/05/31/in-the-know-summer-2023)
- In 2025, Citizens (Florida) moved to keep PAs' names off claim checks, and PAs objected. This is a sign of ongoing friction between PAs and carriers. — [Insurance Journal, Jun 2025](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/southeast/2025/06/23/828683.htm)

### Inferences
- **(estimate)** Nationally there are likely somewhere in the low tens of thousands of PA licenses. Many are non-resident licenses held by the same people, so there are perhaps 5,000–15,000 distinct active PAs. This is an unsourced order of magnitude; verify with NIPR/state lookups.
- A PA collecting 10% on a $20k roof claim earns about $2,000, so a $9.99 report is about 0.5% of the fee. Price is not the barrier; credibility with the carrier is. PAs are adversarial to carriers, which makes them motivated buyers of third-party documentation.
- Most PA files in FL, TX, LA and the Gulf states are wind, hail or hurricane, so a large share of files plausibly has a weather element.

### Gaps
- No national licensed-PA count, no current NAPIA membership count, and no caseload data (files per PA per year) were found.
- No data on which weather tools PAs currently use or how much they spend on them.

## Q2. Independent adjusters, CAT adjusters and staff claims adjusters

### Takeaway
BLS counts about 356,100 claims adjusters, examiners and investigators (2024). This is the largest population, but most are carrier staff whose employer already licenses enterprise weather verification (Verisk Benchmark, Cotality/CoreLogic). Independent and CAT adjusters number in the thousands to tens of thousands. They are organized through about 250–300 NAIIA member firms plus large CAT firms, and no national count was found.

### Cited Findings
- About 356,100 jobs in 2024 for claims adjusters, examiners and investigators. The largest employers are direct P&C carriers (35%), agencies/brokerages (28%) and the federal government (15%). BLS projects a decline of about 5–6% over the following decade, with about 21,600 openings per year. — [BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/claims-adjusters-appraisers-examiners-and-investigators.htm) (figures taken via search snippet; direct fetch failed)
- DataUSA reports a 2024 workforce of 367,687 for the broader group that includes appraisers. — [DataUSA](https://datausa.io/profile/soc/claims-adjusters-appraisers-examiners-investigators)
- NAIIA describes itself as "250 independently owned & vetted adjusting firms with thousands of elite IAs" ([NAIIA on X](https://x.com/NAIIAdjuster)). Insuranceopedia says 300+ member companies ([Insuranceopedia](https://www.insuranceopedia.com/definition/3049/national-association-of-independent-insurance-adjusters-naii)). A 2023 NAIIA pitch deck lists 200+ member offices and 1,400+ independent adjusters and appraisers ([NAIIA 2023 deck](https://naiia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/2023-NAIIA-Pitch-Deck.pdf)). These counts conflict and are self-reported.
- IA firm directories list about 120–123 firms; they are not a census. — [AdjusterPro directory](https://adjusterpro.com/independent-adjusting-firm-directory/); [IApath](https://iapath.com/independent-adjusting-firms/)
- Carriers rely on IA firms for most major disaster work, and a single large firm's roster can exceed 1,000 adjusters. — [insuranceclaimrecoverysupport.com](https://insuranceclaimrecoverysupport.com/catastrophe-adjusting-firms-and-where-to-find-them/) (blog)
- Enterprise tools adjusters use today:
  - Verisk Benchmark provides property-specific weather reports to set the date of loss. Verisk says about 40% of hail claims have an inconsistent date of loss (vendor claim). — [Verisk Benchmark](https://www.verisk.com/products/benchmark/)
  - Cotality/CoreLogic Weather Verification Services markets its Hail Verification Report as the "insurance industry standard". An adjuster testimonial on its site notes that hail sizes and dates vary greatly between report sources. — [CoreLogic WVS](https://www.wvs.corelogic.com/)
  - Verisk publishes a sample Benchmark hail history report. — [Verisk sample PDF](https://www.verisk.com/4a4a83/siteassets/media/downloads/property-estimating/benchmark-hail-history-report-sample.pdf)
- Roofing was about 39–44% of claim assignment volume in Verisk XactInsights data (2023–2025). — [Roofing Contractor, "Insurers Further Tighten Grip"](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/100777-insurers-further-tighten-grip-amid-surging-roof-losses)

### Inferences
- **(estimate)** Of the 356k BLS total, only a minority handle property claims. Many handle auto, workers' comp, health or federal (SSA/VA) claims. The property field-adjuster population is perhaps tens of thousands.
- Staff adjusters are poor direct buyers: their carriers already pay for enterprise weather verification, and individual adjusters rarely buy tools personally.
- Self-employed 1099 IAs and CAT adjusters on deployment are a better fit. They are paid per claim on fee schedules, and some firms may not provide weather tools. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- No national count of licensed independent or CAT adjusters. State license counts, especially Texas and Florida which issue large numbers of non-resident adjuster licenses, would be the source.
- No per-report or per-seat prices for Verisk Benchmark or Cotality WVS (enterprise, unpublished).
- No data on files per adjuster per year with a weather element.

## Q3. Roofing and storm-restoration contractors

### Takeaway
There are about 106,000 roofing contractor businesses (IBISWorld, 2025). Storm and insurance work is a large but unquantified share. These contractors already pay for hail-tracking subscriptions ($349–$649/yr for GAF WeatherHub; $50 to several hundred dollars a month for others) and per-address reports ($40 HailFACTS). A $9.99 report undercuts those per-address prices, but contractors mainly use hail maps for lead generation, not claim evidence.

### Cited Findings
- IBISWorld counts about 105,876 roofing contractor businesses in 2025 and estimates 108,598 for 2026. The market has low concentration; CentiMark is the largest firm. — [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/number-of-businesses/roofing-contractors/198) (paid-database estimate)
- Insurance-funded work held 52.9% of the US disaster restoration services market in 2025. This covers the broader restoration market, not roofing alone. — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-disaster-restoration-services-market)
- US residential roof claims reached about $31B in 2024, up nearly 30% from 2022 (Verisk data via vendor guide; unverified at the primary source). — [plyrium.com](https://plyrium.com/academy/roofing/how-to-build-an-insurance-restoration-business)
- Over 22% of residential roof replacements stemmed from storm damage in 2024 (vendor blog citing III; unverified). — [RoofLink](https://rooflink.com/insights/roofing-industry-statistics/)
- Some contractors are shifting away from insurance work because of claim delays and rising deductibles; one reported 65% retail. — [Roofing Contractor](https://www.roofingcontractor.com/articles/100777-insurers-further-tighten-grip-amid-surging-roof-losses) (anecdotal)
- Roofing Contractor magazine's 2025 State of the Industry report exists, but its insurance-work percentage was not retrievable. — [The Roofing Brief](https://theroofingbrief.com/2026-roofing-contractor-industry-report/)
- Pricing of current tools:
  - GAF WeatherHub (interactive hail map with address-level reports): $349, $499 or $649 per year depending on contractor tier. — [GAF](https://gaf.com/en-us/for-professionals/tools/interactive-hail-map)
  - Anything Weather HailFACTS: $40 per single-address report. — [Anything Weather store](https://store.anythingweather.com/hailfacts)
  - Hail-tracking subscriptions generally cost $49.95 to several hundred dollars per month. — [JLC Online](https://www.jlconline.com/products-tools/exteriors-roofing/hail-tracking-is-key-to-swift-storm-damage-response-by-roofers_o/)
  - HailTrace: $65–$119/month appears in an AccuLynx roundup, but the price may belong to an adjacent product (unconfirmed). — [AccuLynx](https://acculynx.com/making-hail-apps-work-better-for-insurance-restoration-contractors/)
  - HailTrace sells a Weather History Report per address and a "Meteorologist Certified" date-of-loss report; one source lists the meteorologist report at $150. — [HailTrace reports](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports); [HailTrace Meteorologist Certified](https://hailtrace.com/hail-reports/meteorologist-certified)
  - HailStrike offers an 11-page address hail report back to 2011, delivered in minutes. No price was found. — [Roofing Contractor](https://roofingcontractor.com/articles/91723-hailstrike-releases-new-claim-management-system-for-hail-affected-locations)
  - Interactive Hail Maps / Hail Recon: subscription required, with no public price found. One app reviewer called it "overpriced". — [justuseapp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/602877045/hail-recon)
  - WDT HailHistory reports started at $45 in 2012 (outdated). — [WDT](https://email.wdtinc.com/2012/05/07/hailhistory-reports-now-at-dramatically-reduced-price)

### Inferences
- **(estimate)** Maybe 20–40% of roofing firms do meaningful insurance or storm work, which gives a target pool of about 20k–40k firms. This is unsourced and should be validated.
- Contractors who already subscribe to hail maps get unlimited address reports, so a per-report product competes mainly for (a) firms without a subscription, (b) markets outside hail-subscription coverage, and (c) cases where wind/rain/station data are needed, not just hail.
- Note the legal risk the founder flagged: Florida's 2023 definition broadening means contractors who "file claims" can be treated as unlicensed PAs. Marketing must stay "documentation", not claim filing.

### Gaps
- No reliable share of roofers doing insurance work, and no count of "storm chasers".
- Official HailTrace and Interactive Hail Maps prices were not found.
- No figure for reports pulled per contractor per year.

## Q4. Property insurance attorneys, appraisers/umpires, forensic engineers

### Takeaway
First-party property litigation is heavily concentrated in Florida: 71–75% of US homeowners lawsuits in 2022–2023, falling to 41% in 2025 after the reforms. Attorneys and experts currently pay $168+ for NOAA-certified records and $1,000–$10,000+ for forensic meteorology. A cheap report fits their early screening and intake work, not trial evidence. No headcount was found for attorneys, appraisers/umpires or forensic engineers.

### Cited Findings
- Florida's share of national homeowners lawsuits was 70.83% in 2022 and 75.19% in 2023, while its share of national claims was 14.93% and 9.73% (OIR data). — [Business Observer, Jul 2026](https://www.businessobserverfl.com/news/2026/jul/30/report-property-insurance-lawsuits-fall/)
- 2022: Florida had 732,390 closed residential claims, of which 58,395 were litigated. — [FL OIR Jan 2024 report](https://floir.gov/docs-sf/default-source/property-and-casualty/other-property-casualty-reports/january-2024-pclr.pdf?sfvrsn=d8c92a4f_4)
- Florida's share of national homeowners lawsuits fell from 79% (2020) to 41% (2025) after the 2022–23 reforms (end of one-way attorney fees, end of AOB on new policies, shorter deadlines). — [Business Observer](https://www.businessobserverfl.com/news/2026/jul/30/report-property-insurance-lawsuits-fall/); [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/property/florida-homeowners-insurance-lawsuit-share-drops-to-41-after-reform-push-583458.aspx)
- Over a 10-year period, 71% of $51B paid by Florida insurers went to attorneys' fees and public adjusters (OIR figure via Marsh). — [Marsh McLennan Agency](https://www.marshmma.com/us/insights/details/understanding-the-florida-insurance-market.html)
- NOAA NCEI certification of records for court costs $168 for 1–40 pages plus $12 shipping ($180 total); most orders start at $191, take 5–7 business days, and cover only a limited set of products (e.g., LCD, GHCN-D). — [NCEI Data Certification](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/services/certification); [StormRecord blog](https://stormrecord.com/blog/historical-weather-data-insurance-claim/)
- Forensic meteorology: one vendor charges $1,000 for a signed certified report with 3–4 weeks' turnaround. Another source estimates $2,000–$5,000 for basic cases and $10,000+ for complex disputes (unsourced). — [weatherandclimateexpert.com](https://weatherandclimateexpert.com/weather-analysis-for-insurance-claims-expert-insights-for-dispute-resolution/); [J.S. Held on trustworthy weather sources](https://www.jsheld.com/insights/articles/weather-reports-insurance-claims-finding-a-trustworthy-source)
- Low-cost NOAA-derived PDF reports already exist: StormRecord-type products at $9.99 per address/date, and another at $15/report. — [StormRecord](https://stormrecord.com/blog/weather-report-for-insurance-claim/); [inspect.properties](https://inspect.properties/weather)
- Forensic engineers (Haag): there is no public fee schedule. Haag hail assessments use 100 sq ft test squares per roof slope. Contractor blogs cite $200–$500 for "HAAG-certified" inspections, but that is a certified inspector, not a Haag Engineering forensic engagement. — [Haag](https://haagglobal.com/articles/october-2019-blog-post/); [riseroofinghouston.com](https://www.riseroofinghouston.com/roof-inspection-cost/) (contractor blog)
- A federal case shows the date of loss on a claim matching the only hail event in a third-party (Weather Fusion/PointData) report, which illustrates how these reports are used in litigation. — [Justia, N.D. Tex. 3:16-cv-00465](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/texas/txndce/3:2016cv00465/270140/91/0.pdf)

### Inferences
- Attorneys and engineers will want certified or expert-backed evidence at trial, but a $9.99 report can serve as triage at intake ("did anything happen near this address on this date?"). That is a high-frequency, low-stakes use.
- The Florida reforms shrink the attorney segment there; Texas, Louisiana, Colorado and other hail states may be growing relative targets (unverified).
- **(estimate)** Thousands of US lawyers handle first-party property claims, but there is no source.

### Gaps
- No count of first-party property or bad-faith attorneys. Possible sources: Florida Bar sections, Windstorm Insurance Network (WIN) membership, United Policyholders directories.
- No count of appraisers and umpires.
- No count of forensic engineering firms doing weather/roof work, and no Haag fee data.

## Q5. Small insurers, MGAs and TPAs

### Takeaway
There are roughly 600 to over 1,000 US MGAs (AM Best and Conning, 2024), and AAMGA has 500+ corporate members. No TPA count was found. Most of these buy enterprise weather verification bundled with claims platforms (Verisk/Xactware, Cotality). Per-report purchase is plausible only for very small carriers or MGAs with low claim volumes. The project's "do not build" list excludes insurer integrations and APIs, so this segment would be served only through manual per-report purchase.

### Cited Findings
- AM Best: more than 700 MGAs met its reporting threshold, and market research suggests over 1,000 MGAs operate in the US. US MGA direct premium exceeded $102B in 2024. — [AM Best market segment report](https://news.ambest.com/newscontent.aspx?altsrc=177&refnum=266436)
- Conning estimates about 600 US MGAs placing $47B (different definition). — [beinsure.com summary](https://beinsure.com/top-ranking-mga-worldwide/)
- AAMGA represents 500+ corporate members, including MGUs, program administrators and other wholesale professionals. — [Association Adviser / Naylor](https://www.naylor.com/associationadviser/newsletter25009_348/)

### Inferences
- **(estimate)** Low priority for v1: a buyer here wants volume pricing, integrations and an audit trail, all of which are out of scope.

### Gaps
- No count of TPAs, and no count of small property carriers (the NAIC company count would answer this).
- No per-report prices for enterprise tools.

## Q6. Channels to reach these segments (cross-segment)

### Takeaway
Associations and license lists exist for most segments. NAPIA and state PA associations, NAIIA, roofing trade press (Roofing Contractor, RCI, NRCA) and Florida/Texas licensing lookups are the most concrete. Many PA and IA firms publish directories, which helps outbound outreach.

### Cited Findings
- NAPIA member directory ("find a public adjuster"). — [NAPIA](https://www.napia.com/news/napia-members-mobilize-to-support-property-owners-impacted-by-hurricane-francine-in-louisiana-and-su)
- FAPIA, the Florida state PA association. — [FAPIA](https://www.fapia.net/nph.html)
- NAIIA (about 250–300 member IA firms) and public IA firm directories. — [NAIIA on X](https://x.com/NAIIAdjuster); [AdjusterPro directory](https://adjusterpro.com/independent-adjusting-firm-directory/)
- Florida DFS publishes an online license lookup for adjusters. — [Florida DFS public adjusters](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/division/consumers/storm/public-adjusters)
- Roofing Contractor magazine covers hail-tool vendors and runs a State of the Industry survey. — [Roofing Contractor](https://roofingcontractor.com/articles/87631-hailmonitor)
- AAMGA covers MGAs. — [Association Adviser](https://www.naylor.com/associationadviser/newsletter25009_348/)

### Inferences
- The cheapest first test of willingness to pay: direct outreach to Florida/Texas PAs drawn from license lookups and the NAPIA directory, plus roofing forums and Facebook groups. PAs combine high motivation, adversarial posture and contingency income.

### Gaps
- No Reddit or forum threads were found in search on adjusters' pain with weather tools. A targeted manual browse of r/insuranceclaims, r/Roofing and adjuster Facebook groups would be needed.
- No data on conversion or willingness to pay specifically for a $9.99 instant report.
